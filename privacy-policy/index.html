Emdalex Streams — Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 29, 2026 · Developer: Serhii Krotkykh · Contact: sergey.krotkih@gmail.com

This Privacy Policy describes what information the Emdalex Streams application for iOS and Android (“the app”, “we”, “us”) accesses, how it is used, where it is stored, with whom it is shared and how you can control it. It applies to the app and to the web dashboard at emdalex.com/dashboard (section 1.6); the other pages of this website collect nothing.

In short: the app has no servers of its own. It runs entirely on your phone and talks only to Google’s YouTube services, on your behalf and with your permission. We — the developer — never receive, store or see any of your data.

1. Information the app accesses

1.1 Google account information (via Google Sign-In)

When you sign in with Google, the app receives from Google:

  • your name and profile picture,
  • your e-mail address,
  • an OAuth access token and refresh token for the YouTube channel you selected (your own channel, or a brand-account channel you manage).

Why: the name and picture are shown in the app’s side menu so you can see which account is signed in; the streams you create carry a short tag in the last line of their description — your name and an anonymous ID of your phone (a random identifier made by the app on first launch, not linked to the device hardware or your account) — so that members of a shared channel can tell whose stream is whose. The tag is stored on YouTube as part of the stream and is deleted together with it. The tokens let the app call the YouTube API on your behalf.

Where: on iPhone, tokens are stored in the iOS Keychain on your device; on Android, they are managed by Google Play services on your device, and the app does not store them itself; name, e-mail and picture are kept in the app’s memory and local storage on your device only. Nothing is transmitted to us.

1.2 YouTube data (via the YouTube Data API)

The app uses YouTube API Services with the OAuth scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-ssl. With it the app, acting as you:

  • reads the list of live broadcasts on your channel — title, description, scheduled and actual start/end times, privacy status, thumbnail, status (scheduled, live, completed) — to show them on the app’s Scheduled, Ready, Live and Completed tabs;
  • creates live broadcasts and the associated live streams (the RTMP ingestion address your phone streams to) when you schedule or start a stream;
  • updates a broadcast’s title, description, start time and privacy status when you edit it;
  • starts and ends broadcasts (transitions to “live” and “complete”) when you go live or press Finish;
  • deletes a broadcast, its recording and its stream when you delete it in the app.

Why: these are the app’s features — there is no other use. The app does not read your comments, subscriptions, watch history, playlists or any videos other than the live broadcasts listed above, and it does not use YouTube data for advertising, analytics, profiling or training of any kind.

Where: YouTube data is fetched from Google when the app is open and held in memory for display; thumbnails are cached on the device. Nothing is copied to any server of ours. When you sign out or delete the app, all of it is gone from the device.

1.3 Camera and microphone

When you start a stream, the app captures video and audio and sends them directly from your phone to YouTube’s ingestion servers (RTMP). The app does not record, store or analyse this content. The recording that appears on YouTube afterwards is created and stored by YouTube under your account.

Voice on the Finish form. When you tap the microphone on the form shown after a stream (“Tell about the stream”), the app listens until you tap Done or Cancel. Your speech is converted to text on your device by the operating system’s on-device speech recognition (Apple Speech on iPhone, Android’s on-device recognizer), and the title and notes are written from that text by the AI model built into your device (Apple Intelligence on iPhone, Gemini Nano on Android). The audio and the text are not sent to us or to any other service, are not stored, and are used only to fill in the form; nothing is uploaded until you save the title and notes to your stream on YouTube. Speech-recognition permission is asked for only on iPhone, the first time you use it.

Sound control. If you turn on Settings → Sound control (it is off by default), the app listens to the microphone while it is open on the main screen or the camera screen, to notice snaps or claps that start, pause or stop a stream. It only measures how loud the sound is, moment by moment, on your device; the audio is not recorded, not stored and not sent to us or to any other service. While you are on air, your microphone is part of the stream as always. Turn the setting off and the app does not listen outside a stream.

Your subscribers’ e-mail addresses. If you make a list of subscribers (Settings → Subscribers), the addresses you type in or pick from your contacts are kept only on your device. When you pick from your contacts, the system contact picker hands the app only the one address you choose; the app does not read your contacts. The app does not send e-mail itself and sends the addresses to no one, us included: when you send a recording to your subscribers, your own mail app opens with the message, and you send it from your own address.

1.4 Location

With your permission, the app uses your current location once, when you create a stream, to pre-fill the stream description with a street address. You can edit or delete this text before saving. The address becomes part of the stream’s description on YouTube; the app does not store location data and does not use it for anything else. Location is used only while the app is open (“When In Use”), and the app works without it.

1.5 Notifications

With your permission, the app schedules local notifications on your device to remind you when a scheduled stream is due to start. They are generated on the device; no push-notification service is involved.

1.6 Web dashboard

The web dashboard is a read-only page that shows your channel's streams in your browser. It works like the app: you sign in with Google (Google Identity Services) and grant the same YouTube permission; the page then calls the YouTube Data API directly from your browser to list your channel and its live broadcasts, and to read the number of viewers of streams on air. It never creates, changes or deletes anything. The access token is kept only in the memory of the browser tab — not in cookies or local storage — and is gone when you close the tab or tap Sign out. The page has no server of its own and sends nothing to us.

2. Information we collect

None. We have no backend, database, analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking SDKs. The app sends no data to us and no data to any third party other than Google as described in this policy. We cannot see who uses the app, what they stream or where they are.

3. How information is shared

Your data is shared with exactly one party: Google (Google Sign-In, YouTube Data API, YouTube live ingestion and playback), because streaming to YouTube requires it. Google’s handling of your data is governed by the Google Privacy Policy and the YouTube Terms of Service. We do not sell, rent or transfer your data to anyone, and we have nothing to transfer.

4. Data retention and deletion

  • On your device: tokens, profile details and cached thumbnails are removed when you sign out (side menu → Log Out) or delete the app; the app’s settings and the anonymous phone ID are removed when you delete the app.
  • On YouTube: streams and recordings belong to your channel, not to the app. Delete them in the app or in YouTube Studio; deletion is performed in your YouTube account and is final.
  • With us: there is nothing to retain or delete, because we hold no data.
  • Step-by-step instructions: Delete your data.

5. Your choices and controls

  • Revoke the app’s access to your Google account at any time on the Google security settings page (myaccount.google.com/connections). The app then can no longer act on your channel.
  • Sign out in the app’s side menu to remove tokens and profile data from your device.
  • Camera, microphone, location and notification permissions can be changed at any time in iOS Settings → Emdalex Streams or in Android Settings → Apps → Emdalex Streams → Permissions. The app streams only while you have started a stream and only with camera and microphone access granted.
  • Privacy of your streams: new streams are unlisted by default; you choose private, unlisted or public for each stream.

6. Google API Services — Limited Use

Emdalex Streams’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing live-streaming features described above; it is never used for advertising, never sold, never used to train models, and never transferred to anyone except as necessary to provide those features, to comply with the law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with notice to users. Humans never read it, because no copy of it ever reaches us.

7. Security

All communication with Google uses HTTPS (and RTMPS-capable ingestion where YouTube offers it). Tokens are kept in the iOS Keychain on iPhone and by Google Play services on Android, protected by the device lock and the operating system’s security. Because there is no server, there is no server-side data that could be breached.

8. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and requires a Google account that meets Google’s age requirements. We knowingly collect no information from anyone, including children.
