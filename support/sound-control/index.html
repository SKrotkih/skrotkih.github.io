Control the app with snaps or claps

Phone on a tripod a few steps away? You don't have to walk over to it. Snap your fingers or clap — three times to start or stop a stream, twice to pause it — and the app answers with a short sound. Works on iPhone and Android.

Turn it on

  1. Open the side menu → Settings → Sound control → Control by snaps or claps.
  2. Switch it on. The first time, the phone asks to allow the microphone — tap Allow.

It is off by default. If the microphone is not allowed, a red line under the switch says so — tap it to open the phone's settings and allow it there.

The commands

SoundWhereWhat happens
3 snaps or clapsMain screenStarts a stream from the camera. If one of your streams is on the Ready tab, counting down to its start, that one goes live now; otherwise a new stream starts, just like ▶ → Camera.
3 snaps or clapsCamera screenStops the stream, like ■. On air, the stream ends and the Finish form appears — if you don't touch it, it closes by itself after a minute and keeps what is there. Before the stream is on air, it works like Back: nothing was broadcast, and the stream is removed.
2 snaps or clapsCamera screen, on airPauses the stream: viewers see Paused — back soon and hear nothing. Twice more and it resumes.

These are the only commands, and they cannot be changed. On other screens — Settings, the stream form, the player — the phone does not listen.

How to snap so the phone hears you

  • An even rhythm, about half a second apart — snap… snap… snap, as if counting “one, two, three” calmly. Too fast or uneven, and the phone takes it for noise.
  • Sharp and short. A crisp finger snap or a clap works best. Two or three meters from the phone is fine in a quiet room; in a noisy place, come closer or clap louder.
  • Exactly two or three. Four or more in a row are not a command.
  • Hands off the phone. While you touch the screen, and for a few seconds after, the phone does not listen — the knocks of a phone in your hand are not commands. Each screen also waits 3 seconds after it opens before it listens.
Try it before you leave the phone. Set it on the tripod, step to where you will stand and snap three times on the main screen: two notes going up, and the stream starts. Snap three times again to stop it — before it is on air, so nothing is broadcast.

What the sounds mean

  • Two notes going up — understood, doing it.
  • Two notes going down — understood, but not possible now. For example: two snaps before the stream is on air (there is nothing to pause yet), or three snaps on the main screen when a stream cannot start — the banner on the screen says why.
  • No sound — not heard as a command. Try again, a little louder and in an even rhythm.

The answer is short and quiet enough not to disturb the stream, but viewers may hear it on air. Its volume follows the phone's volume.

When the phone listens

  • Only while the app is open on the main screen or the camera screen and Sound control is on. Not in the background, not with the screen locked.
  • While it listens, the phone shows that the microphone is in use: an orange dot on iPhone, a green one on Android. That is expected.
  • Not for streams from a video file — those use no microphone.

Good to know

  • The phone hears the rhythm, not what makes the sound. A snap, a clap, a knock on the table — all count the same. So does a slow tapping on a keyboard right next to the phone, or a door knocked three times. If you are working next to the phone and don't need it, turn Sound control off.
  • Pause works for camera streams only, as with the button.
  • For a stream that should start at a set time with nobody near the phone, use Wake me for my streams as well.

Your privacy

The phone only measures how loud each moment is, to find short sharp sounds. Nothing is recorded, stored or sent anywhere — not to us, not to any service. Before you go live, the sound does not leave the phone; on air, the stream carries your microphone as always. See the Privacy Policy.
