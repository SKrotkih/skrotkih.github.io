Using Emdalex Streams

Emdalex Streams sends live video from your phone to your own YouTube channel. This page walks you through the app from the first sign-in to a finished recording.

Need help? Write to sergey.krotkih@gmail.com. Please include your phone model, iOS or Android version and, if possible, a screenshot — we usually answer within two business days.

Before you start

You need two things:

  1. A Google account with a YouTube channel. Any Gmail address is a Google account. No channel yet? Just sign in: the app notices that the account has no channel and helps you create one in a minute — see No YouTube channel yet. Step-by-step instructions are on Your own YouTube channel.
  2. Live streaming switched on for that channel. YouTube turns it on once per channel, after a phone verification and a wait of up to 24 hours. The same page shows how: Enable live streaming.

Want to stream together with your family or a group into one shared channel? Then the group's organizer sets up the channel once: A channel for your group. Members only need their own Google account.

Sign in

Signing in answers two different questions, so Google may ask you to choose twice:

  • Who are you? — the Google account (your e-mail) you sign in with.
  • Which YouTube channel should the app work with? — your own channel, or a group channel you were invited to.

Steps

  1. Open the app, swipe through the short introduction and tap Get started (or Skip).
  2. Tap Sign in with Google. On iPhone the system asks whether the app may use google.com to sign in — tap Continue. On Android Google's sign-in page opens in a browser tab over the app (accounts.google.com at the top).
  3. Choose an account. Pick your Google account (your e-mail). If it is not in the list, tap Use another account and enter the e-mail and password.
  4. Choose your account or a brand account. If your Google account has access to more than one YouTube channel, Google shows a second list. It looks similar to the first one, but now you are choosing the channel:
    • to stream to your own channel, tap your name again (the first line, with your e-mail);
    • to stream to a group channel, tap the group's name.
    If you have only one channel, Google skips this step.
  5. Allow access — tick the box. Google shows Emdalex Streams wants access to your Google Account and, under Select what Emdalex Streams can access, one line: See, edit, and permanently delete your YouTube videos, ratings, comments and captions. Tick the box on the right of that line — it may be empty — then scroll down and tap Continue. The app needs this permission to create, start and stop streams on your channel. If the box is left empty, the sign-in does not finish: on iPhone the app shows YouTube permissions — tap Grant and tick the box; on Android the app asks you to sign in again — tap Sign in with Google and tick the box this time.

The channel you are signed in to is shown at the top of the side menu (tap ☰ in the top-left corner). To switch to another channel, open the side menu, tap Log Out and sign in again, choosing the other channel in step 4.

No YouTube channel yet

If the Google account you signed in with has no YouTube channel, the app shows This Google account has no YouTube channel yet instead of the main screen. You are already signed in — nothing to repeat:

  1. Tap Create a YouTube channel. YouTube opens (in the YouTube app or the browser) on the page for creating a channel, with your Google account already signed in.
  2. Check the name and the handle YouTube suggests — your own name is fine — and tap Create channel.
  3. Come back to Emdalex Streams. The app notices the new channel by itself and opens the main screen.

Before the first stream the new channel also needs live streaming switched on. Signed in with the wrong account, or meant to use a group channel? Tap Use another account and, in step 4, pick the right one.

You stay signed in: next time the app opens straight on the main screen.

The main screen

At the bottom there are four tabs — a stream moves through them from left to right:

  • Scheduled — streams planned for later. The + button in the header creates a new one.
  • Ready — streams that start within the next 15 minutes, with a countdown. A stream that was not started in time moves to Expired (red date); tap it to set a new time.
  • Live — streams on air right now, from this phone or any other phone signed in to the same channel.
  • Completed — finished streams. Their recordings are on YouTube; search them by title or sort by date.

Your own streams are marked with a dot before the title: green for public, blue for unlisted, red for private. Only you can start, edit, finish or delete them; on a group channel you see the others' streams too, but cannot change them. To delete one of your streams, swipe its row to the left.

The side menu (☰) has Quick Start (the introduction again), Open the guide (this page), Settings (an alarm for your scheduled streams, where group streams play, the frame rate of camera streams) and Log Out.

Go live right now

  1. Hold the phone the way you want the video to look — upright or sideways. The orientation is fixed when the stream starts and stays the same until the end.
  2. Tap the round red ▶ button above the tabs. The first time, allow access to the camera, microphone and (optionally) location.
  3. The camera screen opens. While the stream gets ready, a ring turns at the bottom and the label says Preparing, then Connecting. Going live usually takes 10–30 seconds — YouTube needs that time to receive the first video.
  4. When you are on air, the ring gives way to the round stop button, the red timer starts at the top and the label says You're live. Tap ■ to end the stream. The app makes no sound — nothing leaks into the stream.

The stream is titled Emdalex Streams; if you allowed location, its description holds the street address. You can change both when you finish.

Changed your mind before you are on air? Tap Cancel (or Back): the stream has not started yet, so it is simply removed from YouTube.

Pause a stream

YouTube has no pause, so the app makes one. On air, tap Pause (left of the stop button): viewers see a black screen saying Paused — back soon and hear nothing; the camera and the microphone are off. The stream itself goes on — the timer keeps counting, and the stream length limit too (90 minutes unless raised in Settings). Tap Resume and the camera is back in the same stream; the recording on YouTube is one video with the pause in it. To end the stream, resume first. Pause works for camera streams.

Schedule a stream

  1. Open the Scheduled tab and tap + in the header (or Schedule a stream when the list is empty).
  2. Enter a Title (required) and, if you like, a Description. If you allowed location, the description already holds the street address — edit or delete it as you wish.
  3. Choose Who can watch:
    • Unlisted (default) — only people with the link or with access to the channel;
    • Private — only you and accounts you invite on YouTube;
    • Public — anyone on YouTube; the app asks you to confirm.
  4. Set the Start time (at least one minute from now) and the Duration.
  5. Tap Save. The stream appears on Scheduled, and 15 minutes before the start it moves to Ready.

At the start time the app goes live by itself if it is open. If the app is in the background, you get a Time to go live notification — tap it and the stream starts. You can also start earlier: on the Ready tab tap GO LIVE FROM CAMERA on your stream. Phone on a tripod and you won't be holding it at the start? Let it wake up for the stream.

Wake the phone for your stream

Turn on side menu → Settings → My scheduled streams → Wake me for my streams (off by default). Then, at the start time of each stream you scheduled, the phone rings an alarm and wakes up — even when it is locked, the screen is off or the app is closed:

  • iPhone (iOS 26 or later): a full-screen Time to go live alarm, on the Lock Screen too. Tap Go live, unlock the phone, and the app opens and starts the stream. iPhone does not let any app turn on the camera by itself, so this one tap is needed. The first time, iPhone asks whether the app may set alarms — tap Allow.
  • Android: a red Time to go live screen over the lock screen with a 10-second countdown. When it reaches zero, the stream starts by itself — no need to unlock the phone. Tap Go live now to start at once, or Cancel to keep the stream waiting on the Ready tab. The phone has to allow notifications, Alarms & reminders and, on Android 14 and later, full-screen notifications: if one is missing, a red line under the switch says which — tap it to open the right page of the phone's settings.

No alarm while the app is open — it starts the stream itself, as always — or for a stream switched to ⋯ → Don't start automatically. The reminder a minute before the start stays. Set the phone up before you leave it: on a tripod, on a charger, camera pointing where you want it.

To change a scheduled stream, tap it. Two of your own streams cannot overlap in time — the app tells you if they do.

The stream form, field by field

The New stream and Edit stream screens. The i button at the top of the form opens this section.

Title — required
The name viewers see on YouTube. Up to 100 characters; the signs < and > are not allowed. While it is empty the field is outlined in red and Save stays off.
Description — optional
Shown under the video on YouTube and in the app's lists. If you allowed location, the app fills in the street address where you are — keep it, change it or delete it. On YouTube you will also see a last line starting with emdalex: — it marks the stream as yours in the app; please leave it.
Who can watch
Unlisted (default) — only people with the link or with access to the channel; Private — only you and the accounts you invite on YouTube; Public — anyone on YouTube, in search and on your channel. Choosing Public asks you to confirm.
Start at a scheduled time?
On: the stream is planned for the Start date and time below — at least one minute from now. Off: it starts as soon as you press Stream.
Duration
How long the stream is planned for: from 3 minutes up to the stream length limit, or Unlimited (default). The app stops any stream after the limit — 90 minutes, or up to 4 hours if raised in Settings. Two of your own streams cannot overlap; an Unlimited one counts as the full limit.
Add to Calendar — off by default
After Save, your phone's calendar opens with this stream (title, time, link) — tap Add there. Only for a stream with a start time. On iPhone it rings a minute before the start; on Android the calendar's usual reminder applies. A stream changed later is not updated in the calendar — turn the switch on again when you edit it. Another member's stream: ⋯ → Add to Calendar (more).
Category — Other by default
What the stream is about: Home, Work / School, Travel, Friends, Pets, Hobby, Fun or Other. It helps to find the recording later on the Completed tab (see Find recordings by category). Only the author of a stream chooses it; you can also choose or change it on the form that opens when you finish the stream.
Advanced settings — you rarely need them
  • Viewer delay — how far viewers are behind you: Normal (15–30 s, best quality), Low (5–10 s, default), Ultra low (2–5 s, up to 1080p, no rewinding).
  • Is it child-directed? — turn on if the stream is made for children; YouTube then limits comments and ads.
  • Start / stop automatically — YouTube goes live when the video arrives and ends the stream about a minute after it stops. Keep both on.
  • Closed captions — only if you send captions from separate software.
  • DVR — viewers can pause and rewind during the stream.
  • Embedded player — the stream may play inside other apps and websites. Group (Brand) channels often may not; the app handles that by itself.
  • Record automatically — keep on: the recording is what you find on the Completed tab.
  • Monitor stream — a private preview for you in YouTube Studio; usually not needed.
  • Reuse this stream key — for external encoders; leave off.

Then tap Save. If the time overlaps another of your streams, the app tells you which one.

Stream videos from your library

Instead of the camera you can broadcast videos from your phone's library — for example, recordings made earlier.

Even quicker: share them. In Photos, Gallery or Files select one or several videos, tap Share → Emdalex Streams — the app opens and streams them right away. Share a video straight to your YouTube
  1. Tap the round red ▶ button above the tabs and choose Video file (or, for a scheduled stream on the Ready tab, ⋯ → Go live from a video file).
  2. Pick one video or several. They play in the order you picked them.
  3. The app connects to YouTube showing the first frame of the first video. As soon as you are on air, the videos play from the very beginning, one after another, with 3 seconds of black screen between them.
  4. When the last video ends, the stream finishes by itself.

The first video sets the shape of the stream (upright or sideways); videos of another shape are shown with black bars. A stream runs at most 90 minutes (or the longer limit set in Settings), so the app takes the videos in order while they fit — one long video is always taken (the stream simply stops at the limit); the rest are left out and the app tells you. On Android a video whose sound is recorded differently from the first one's plays without sound — the app tells you that too.

Finish a stream

  1. Tap the round stop button at the bottom of the camera screen (or Finish stream on the Live tab).
  2. The stream stops and is saved on your YouTube channel. A form opens where you can change the Title, the Notes, the Category and the Thumbnail (from your library or a new photo) — or just say what the stream was about. Tap Done — your changes are saved; leave the thumbnail as it is and the app picks a frame from the video. The form also closes by itself after a minute.
  3. The recording appears on the Completed tab a moment later.

If the connection drops, the app keeps trying for a while; if the video does not get through, YouTube ends the stream and the app tells you.

Tell about the stream by voice

No need to type on the Finish form. Tap Tell about the stream (the red microphone at the top of the form) and speak:

  1. Say the title first — for example “Copenhagen, May 2026” — then pause and tell what happened: “Me and my son are walking around the Marina yacht club.” Your words appear on the screen as you speak.
  2. Tap Done. In a moment the Title, the Notes and, when it is clear, the Category are filled in, replacing what was there.
  3. Read them, change anything you like, and tap Done on the form.

Cancel leaves the form as it was. Recording stops by itself after a minute. While you speak, the form does not close by itself.

Everything happens on your phone. Your voice is turned into text by the phone itself, and the title and notes are written by the AI model built into the phone — Apple's on iPhone, Google's Gemini Nano on Android. Nothing is sent to us or to any AI service, and nothing is stored.

  • iPhone: the title and notes are written by Apple Intelligence (iPhone 15 Pro or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on). On other iPhones your words go into the Notes as you said them and the first phrase becomes the title — that is why it helps to say the title first. The first time, iPhone asks to allow Speech Recognition and the microphone.
  • Android: Android 12 or later with speech recognition on the device. Phones with Gemini Nano (for example Pixel 9 or later, Galaxy S26) write the title and notes; on the others your words go into the Notes as said and the first phrase becomes the title.
  • Speak the language your phone is set to. No microphone button? Your phone cannot recognize speech in its language without the internet — type as usual.

Watch streams and recordings

  • Your own channel: tap a stream on the Live or Completed tab and it plays right in the app. Use the arrow buttons on the sides of the player to move to the previous or the next recording.
  • A group channel: YouTube does not let apps play group-channel streams inside them, so they open on YouTube. The first time, the app asks where — in a built-in browser or in the YouTube app. You can change this later in the side menu → Settings → Watch group streams.

When someone in your group goes live, the app tells you — how and how soon is explained in Notifications when a stream starts. Live video on YouTube runs about 10–30 seconds behind; that is normal.

Recordings you have not watched yet

Every recording that appears on the Completed tab while you are away from the app — yours too — is marked as not watched yet:

  • a green dot to the left of its picture;
  • a number on the Completed tab — how many recordings have the dot;
  • the same number on the app icon on your home screen. On iPhone it is updated whenever iPhone wakes the app in the background, so it may lag a little; on Android it comes with a quiet notification “3 new recordings” (some phones show the number on the icon, others only a dot).

Opening the app does not clear them: a recording loses its dot when you open it to watch — in the app, in the browser or in the YouTube app — and the numbers go down with it. To clear them all at once, tap ⋯ on the Completed tab → Mark all as watched (only your marks go; your watch history stays).

No number on the iPhone icon? Check Settings → Notifications → Emdalex Streams → Badges.

Find recordings by category

On the Completed tab tap ⋯ at the top right → Categories. The categories are shown as buttons above the list: tap one or several, and the list shows only their recordings — tap again to remove one. With none selected, every recording is shown. Each recording shows its category after the date. Search and Sort work there too; the back arrow returns to all recordings.

A recording gets its category from the stream form or the finish form; streams started with the ▶ button or shared from Photos are Other unless you choose one when you finish. Other is not offered as a filter. Recordings made before categories existed count as Other.

Notifications when a stream starts

Emdalex Streams has no server of its own: your phone talks only to YouTube. That keeps your streams between you and YouTube — but it also means nobody can send your phone a message the moment another member goes live. The app has to find out by itself, and an app that is asleep cannot do that at any moment it likes. So the app does what can be done without a server:

  • Scheduled streams — on time (iPhone). Whenever the app sees the list of streams, it asks iPhone to show “Anna is going live” at the start time of every stream another member has scheduled. iPhone shows it itself — even when the app is asleep or closed. Tap it: the app opens on the Live tab, and as soon as the stream is on air (usually within half a minute) it is outlined there.
    The app has to have seen the stream once after it was scheduled: when you open the app, or when iPhone wakes it in the background. A stream moved or deleted in the meantime is updated the same way.
  • Streams started right away (the ▶ button, videos shared from Photos) cannot be known in advance. iPhone wakes the app in the background now and then and the app checks the list — but when is up to iPhone: after a few minutes or a few hours, and not at all in Low Power Mode or after you swipe the app away. So such a notification may come late, or not come.
  • While the app is open you see a banner within half a minute, and the Live tab shows the number of streams on air.
  • Your own scheduled streams remind you with a sound a minute before the start (Your stream starts in a minute) and with Time to go live at the start time — or with an alarm, if you turned on Wake me for my streams.
  • Put a member's stream in your calendar. On the Scheduled or Ready tab tap ⋯ on another member's stream → Add to Calendar: your phone's calendar opens with the stream (title, time, link) — tap Add there. The calendar reminds you whatever the app is doing: on iPhone a minute before the start, on Android with the calendar's usual reminder. If the stream is moved later, the calendar is not updated — add it again. For your own streams use the Add to Calendar switch on the stream form.
  • Android: the same notification at the start time of every scheduled stream. On the minute when the app may set alarms: on Android 12 and later it asks once — tap Allow and turn on Allow setting alarms and reminders (or later: Settings → Apps → Special app access → Alarms & reminders). Without it the notification comes within 10 minutes of the start. And Android lets the app check the list every 15 minutes in the background, so a stream started right away is announced too — “Anna is live now”, up to about 15 minutes late. The app asks once for permission to show notifications.

Notifications about other members' streams arrive silently — a phone ringing next to someone who is streaming would be heard on air. Only your own stream sounds: the reminder a minute before it and the alarm at its start, when nothing is on air yet.

Want the group to be told on time? Schedule the stream instead of starting it with ▶, and the earlier the better: each phone learns about it the next time its app is opened or woken in the background. And allow notifications for Emdalex Streams (iPhone Settings → Notifications; Android Settings → Apps → Emdalex Streams → Notifications).

No notifications on Android? Some phones (Xiaomi, Huawei, some Samsung models) stop apps in the background to save battery. Open Settings → Apps → Emdalex Streams → Battery and allow it to run in the background (the item is called Unrestricted, No restrictions or similar).

The web dashboard

To look through your group's streams on a computer, open emdalex.com/dashboard and tap Sign in with Google. Choose the same Google account and the same channel as in the app.

  • On air now — big cards at the top, with the number of viewers.
  • Scheduled · Ready · Live · Completed — the same tabs as in the app, with search by title and filters by member and by category.
  • Click a stream to watch it on YouTube (a new tab).
  • The page updates by itself every minute while it is open.

It is a view only: scheduling, going live, editing and deleting stay in the app. Everything happens in your browser — there is no server in between, the sign-in lasts only while the tab is open (Sign out ends it), and nothing is stored. Google asks for the same permission as the app.

Limits on streaming

Two kinds of limits apply to every stream: the app's own (quality, length, scheduling) and YouTube's, which belong to the channel. In a group all members stream to one channel, so YouTube's limits — and penalties — are shared by the whole group.

The app's limits

  • Picture from the camera: 720p — 1280×720 sideways or 720×1280 upright — at 30 frames per second, up to 2.5 Mbit/s. 1080p and 60 frames per second can be chosen in Settings (up to 4.5 Mbit/s for 1080p; more with 60 fps). On a weak connection the app lowers the quality instead of dropping the stream; on a hot phone it lowers it too (to 1.5 or 1 Mbit/s) until the phone cools down.
  • Sound: stereo AAC, 128 kbit/s.
  • Videos from your library: streamed at most at 720p (the longer side at most 1280 pixels; 1920 with 1080p in Settings) and 30 frames per second, whatever their own quality — a 4K video is scaled down. The first video sets the shape of the stream.
  • Length: at most 90 minutes on air per stream (up to 4 hours if raised in Settings), a pause included. The recording stays on YouTube when the app stops the stream.
  • Orientation: a camera stream keeps the orientation it started with until the end.
  • Scheduling: at least one minute ahead; two of your own streams cannot overlap (an Unlimited stream counts as the full length limit). A stream reaches Ready 15 minutes before its start.
  • Title: up to 100 characters, without < and >.
  • Leaving the app: the stream needs the app on screen. If the connection drops, the app keeps reconnecting for up to 45 seconds. On Android, answering a call or switching to another app stops the stream (the recording up to that moment is kept); on iPhone a phone call only pauses the picture and sound.
  • The app's daily request limit: YouTube gives the app a fixed number of requests per day, shared by all its users. If it runs out, a new stream cannot start until the limit resets at midnight Pacific time (about 10:00 in Kyiv) — the app shows the exact time. A stream already on air goes on.

Limits you can raise in Settings

Side menu → Settings → Streaming. The defaults are the recommended values; change them only when you need to.

  • Frame rate — 30 fps (default) or 60 fps for fast motion. More data and battery; a dim room looks grainier.
  • Picture quality — 720p (default) or 1080p. 1080p uses about twice the mobile data (around 2 GB per hour instead of 1), needs a strong upload (6 Mbit/s or more) and warms the phone faster.
  • Stream length — 90 minutes (default), 2, 3 or 4 hours. Long streams drain the battery and heat the phone, and use more of the app's daily request limit shared by all users.

These settings are kept on the phone only — each phone has its own, and nothing is sent anywhere. A change applies to the next stream.

YouTube's limits on a channel

  • Live streaming must be on. The channel has to be verified, without live streaming restrictions in the past 90 days, and the person streaming must be at least 16 (younger only with an adult visibly present). The first switch-on can take up to 24 hours.
  • New live streams per day. YouTube limits how many live streams a channel can create in 24 hours; it does not publish the number. Every stream counts — also one cancelled before it went live — and in a group everyone's streams count together. When the limit is reached, wait 24 hours.
  • Streams at the same time. At most 10 active streams per channel. The app gives every stream its own stream key, so the per-key limit (3) does not matter.
  • Recordings. YouTube keeps a recording automatically if the stream is shorter than 12 hours — always the case with the app's limit of at most 4 hours.
  • Custom thumbnails need a verified channel and have a daily limit; when it is reached, set the thumbnail later in YouTube Studio.
  • Playing group streams inside apps (embedding) is an advanced YouTube feature; without it group streams open on YouTube.

What gives a channel higher limits

YouTube has three levels of features. You can see yours in YouTube Studio → Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility.

  • Standard — any channel without active strikes: live streaming, custom thumbnails, each with a small daily limit.
  • Intermediate — after a phone verification: higher daily limits for live streams and thumbnails.
  • Advanced — after enough channel history (often granted automatically) or a verification with an ID or a video: higher limits still, and live streams may be embedded. Keep following YouTube's rules to keep it.

For a group channel the verification is done by its owner in the channel's settings.

What makes the limits stricter

  • Community Guidelines. The first violation is usually a warning. A strike blocks uploads and live streams, scheduled ones included, for one week; a second strike within 90 days blocks them for two weeks; a third within 90 days may remove the channel. Each strike expires after 90 days; deleting the video does not remove it. A strike for a restricted live stream blocks live streaming for 14 days.
  • Copyright. YouTube scans every live stream, including streams of library videos. Music, TV or someone else's stream in the background can replace your picture with a placeholder, interrupt the stream or end it — sometimes even with licensed music.
  • Minors. A stream with children under 16 and no adult in the picture may be removed or lose its chat, and the channel may temporarily lose live streaming.
  • In a group, one member's violation restricts the channel for everyone. Agree on the rules within the group.

What you can do

  • Longer than 90 minutes: raise the limit in Settings (up to 4 hours), or finish the stream and start a new one — you get two recordings.
  • Daily limit of new streams: avoid test starts you cancel; verify the channel's phone number to reach the intermediate level.
  • Better picture: use Wi-Fi or a strong mobile signal; keep the phone out of the sun and, on long streams, take it out of a thick case. For a sharper picture choose 1080p in Settings — on a strong connection only.
  • The app's daily request limit: try again after the reset, and let us know — we can ask Google for a larger limit.
  • Penalties cannot be got around. While live streaming is restricted, do not stream through another channel: YouTube may close the account for that.

YouTube's rules change from time to time; the details are on its help pages: Get started with live streaming, Feature access, Community Guidelines strikes, Copyright issues with live streams.

Questions and problems

Can I open a stream from a YouTube link?

Yes: in the YouTube app (or any app with a link) tap Share → Emdalex Streams, and the app opens on that stream. See Open a stream from YouTube.

The app says “This Google account has no YouTube channel yet”

You signed in with a Google account that has no YouTube channel. Tap Create a YouTube channel, create it on YouTube with the same account (it takes a minute), then come back to the app — it notices the new channel by itself, no need to sign in again. A new channel also needs live streaming switched on before the first stream. Signed in with the wrong account? Tap Use another account.

I created a channel for my group, but the app still says there is no channel

A group (brand) channel is a separate account: the app sees it only if you pick it when signing in. Tap Use another account, sign in with your own Google account and, on Choose your account or a brand account, tap the group channel. See For members: accept and sign in.

The app says “Live streaming is not enabled yet”

YouTube has not switched on live streaming for the channel yet. Follow Enable live streaming; after the 24-hour wait, just open the app again.

I don't see the group channel when signing in

The group's organizer must invite you as a Manager on the Brand Accounts page, and you must accept the invitation in the e-mail from Google. People added through Permissions in YouTube Studio do not see the channel in apps. See Invite the members.

My phone is full — can I film with the app instead?

Yes: go live, and the video goes straight to your own YouTube channel — nothing is stored on the phone. Keep in mind that it is a live stream: it needs an internet connection, and the recording has the stream's quality (720p, or 1080p if chosen in Settings; lower on a weak connection), not your camera's full quality. Your channel also needs live streaming switched on beforehand (once, up to 24 hours) — set it up before the day you need it.

Where are my videos?

On your YouTube channel. The app stores nothing on the phone and has no servers of its own. Open the YouTube app or YouTube Studio to watch, share, edit, download or delete any recording.

Who can see my streams?

New streams are unlisted: only people with the link or with access to the channel can watch them, and they never appear in search or recommendations. You can choose private or public when scheduling a stream. A finished stream can be changed later: on the Completed tab tap ⋯ → Who can watch…. Only the phone that streamed it can do that. Before making a stream public the app asks once more: you can make it unlisted or private again later, but what people have already watched, saved or shared can't be taken back.

A stream did not start at its scheduled time

A scheduled stream starts only on the phone of the person who created it, and only while the app is open, after that person taps the Time to go live notification, or with Wake me for my streams turned on. Make sure notifications are allowed for Emdalex Streams in the phone's Settings and that the phone is not in Low Power Mode (iPhone) or Battery Saver (Android).

The connection dropped during a stream

If the mobile network drops for a moment, the app reconnects by itself: Connection lost, reconnecting… appears under the status, and the stream goes on on YouTube. The app keeps trying for up to 45 seconds; if the network is not back by then, the stream is stopped and the recording up to that moment stays on YouTube. On a slow connection the app lowers the video quality instead of stopping (Weak connection); on a hot phone it does the same to let it cool down. The screen stays on during a stream, so the phone can stand on a tripod untouched.

The viewer count shows fewer people than are watching — or none

The number next to the eye at the top of the camera screen comes from YouTube, and YouTube counts different viewers:

  • People watching while signed in to the same Google account as the phone that streams are counted together as one viewer — or not at all, when they watch the channel's own stream as the channel itself.
  • Several phones of one person signed in to the same account count as one.
  • The number changes with a delay of up to a minute, and it is hidden until YouTube has one to show.
  • If the viewer count is hidden for the video in YouTube Studio, the app cannot show it either.

To check it, watch the stream from another Google account, or open its link on a computer in a private (incognito) window.

My stream is not marked as mine on another phone

The app recognizes your streams by an ID it keeps on the phone and adds to the stream description on YouTube. Each phone has its own ID, even with the same Google account or Apple ID, so a stream counts as yours only on the phone that created it. After reinstalling the app, or for a stream left on another phone, use Edit or Delete on it: the app offers to make it yours first.

The thumbnail was not saved

YouTube allows custom thumbnails only on verified channels — verify yours at youtube.com/verify. If YouTube says it received too many thumbnails recently, set it later in YouTube Studio.

I deleted a stream in the app — is it gone from YouTube?

Yes. Deleting a stream removes the broadcast, its recording and its stream key from YouTube. This cannot be undone.

Sign-in keeps failing

Open the side menu, tap Log Out and sign in again. On Google's permission screen make sure the box next to See, edit, and permanently delete your YouTube videos… is ticked — see Sign in, step 5.

The app opened on the sign-in screen by itself

Google no longer accepts the app's access — for example it was removed on myaccount.google.com/connections, or after a password change. Tap Sign in with Google and go through the steps again. A lost internet connection never signs you out.

Remove the app's access to your Google Account

When you signed in, Google asked you to allow Emdalex Streams to access your YouTube account. You can take that permission away at any time. The app keeps nothing anywhere else, so this is all there is to remove.

Log Out in the app's side menu already removes the access (that is why Google asks for the permission again at the next sign-in). If you no longer have the phone, or want to make sure, remove it in Google's settings of the account you chose as the channel when signing in:

  • Your own channel — your Google account: in a browser go to myaccount.google.com/connections and sign in if asked. (From the Google or Gmail app: tap your picture → Manage your Google Account → Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services.)
  • A group channel — the group's Brand Account, not your own account: go to myaccount.google.com/brandaccounts, choose the group channel → View general account info (the group's name is now at the top) → Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services.

Then tap Emdalex Streams → Delete all → Confirm.

Each channel you signed in to has its own entry, so removing it from one channel does not affect the others. Your streams and recordings stay on YouTube. The app notices within a minute or two and shows Your Google sign-in has ended; if you sign in again later, Google simply asks for the permission once more.
