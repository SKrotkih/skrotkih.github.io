Share a video straight to your YouTube

Any video on your phone can go to your own YouTube channel in two taps: Share → Emdalex Streams. The app opens and streams it at once — one video or several, one after another. When it ends, the recording is on YouTube and the video can leave your phone. Works on iPhone and Android.

Why it is useful

  • The phone is full. Share the videos you want to keep, wait until they are on YouTube, then delete them from the phone. They stay on your channel, ready to watch or download later.
  • A video someone sent you. Saved a video from WhatsApp, Telegram or Messages? Share it from Photos (or straight from the chat, where the app offers it) and the whole family sees it on the group channel.
  • Filmed with another app. The phone's own camera, a dashcam app, a screen recording — whatever made the video, sharing it is enough.
  • A playlist in one go. Select several videos — a day of a trip, a match, a school concert in parts — and they become one stream and one recording.

On iPhone

  1. In Photos open a video, or tap Select and pick several in the order you want them to play.
  2. Tap Share and choose Emdalex Streams.
    Not in the row of apps? Scroll the row to the end, tap More and turn Emdalex Streams on (tap Edit to move it to Favorites so it is always first).
  3. The sheet shows Preparing video… for a moment while the videos are handed over, then Emdalex Streams opens and goes live.

The same works in Files and in other apps that share a video file.

On Android

  1. In Google Photos or your Gallery open a video, or press and hold to select several in the order you want them to play.
  2. Tap Share and choose Emdalex Streams. If it is not shown, tap More and find it; press and hold it to pin it to the top.
  3. Emdalex Streams opens and goes live.

The same works in Files and in other apps that share a video file.

What happens in the app

  • The app is already open (or in the background): the stream starts right away — exactly as if you had tapped the red ▶ button and chosen Video file. Before going live you still have a few seconds to tap Cancel.
  • The app was closed: it starts, signs you in if needed, loads your streams and then asks Stream this video? — tap Go Live or Cancel.
  • You are already streaming: the current stream is not touched; the app says to finish it first and share the video again.
  • Another stream of yours is due at this time: the app says which one, the same as for the ▶ button.
  • When the last video ends, the usual Finish form opens: change the title, notes or cover, or tap Done. The recording is in the Completed tab.

Good to know

  • It is a live stream, not an upload. A video plays in real time: a 10-minute video takes 10 minutes, and the phone needs the internet all that time. Keep the app open — the screen does not go dark by itself.
  • Quality: the recording has the stream's quality — 720p, or 1080p if chosen in Settings; lower on a weak connection. A 4K video is scaled down.
  • Length: a stream runs at most 90 minutes, or the longer limit set in Settings. Several videos are taken in order while they fit; one long video is always taken and simply stops at the limit. The app tells you what was left out.
  • Several videos: they play in the order you selected them, with 3 seconds of black screen between them; the first one sets the shape of the stream (upright or sideways).
  • Only videos: photos are left out — Live Photos too, even with the Loop or Bounce effect that makes them look like a video. On iPhone the app says how many were left out; on Android the share sheet offers the app only when the selection is videos.
  • Who sees it: the stream is unlisted, like any new stream in the app — only people with the link can watch. The title is the usual one for a stream started now; the notes say Imported from library.
